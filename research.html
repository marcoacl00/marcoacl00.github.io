Research Interests

Theoretical and computational jet physics across collision systems, from precision QCD in vacuum to jets and heavy quarks in the quark-gluon plasma.

In-medium QCD splittings

When a highly energetic quark or gluon crosses the quark-gluon plasma, its splitting pattern is modified by multiple interactions with the medium. I work on first-principles calculations of in-medium parton splitting functions, going beyond the soft-gluon, large-Nc and harmonic-oscillator approximations commonly used in the field, all at once, and extending them to heavy quarks and to more realistic physics scenarios.

  • jet quenching
  • splitting functions
  • heavy quarks

Parton showers

Parton showers describe how quarks and gluons produced in a hard collision radiate and evolve down to the scale where hadrons form, and they are at the core of every general-purpose event generator. I work within the PanScales framework on showers with higher logarithmic accuracy and their matching to fixed-order calculations, as well as on tuning non-perturbative Monte Carlo parameters with machine learning.

  • PanScales
  • resummation
  • fixed-order matching
  • ML tuning

Jet quenching phenomenology

Jets are among the most powerful probes of the quark-gluon plasma created in heavy-ion collisions at RHIC and the LHC. I am interested in connecting first-principles calculations to measurable jet quenching observables.

  • heavy-ion collisions
  • jet substructure
  • energy correlators

Computational methods

Many problems in QCD, such as the evolution of a parton crossing a medium, are intrinsically high-dimensional. I explore tensor networks and other numerical approaches — GPU computing and machine learning — to make them tractable, and I have supervised several student projects in this direction.

  • tensor networks
  • HPC / GPUs
  • machine learning
